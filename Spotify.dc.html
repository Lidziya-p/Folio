Spotify Memories

A four-week concept project in collaboration between Spotify and the Estonian Academy of Arts interaction design course, presented at Spotify HQ in Stockholm.

Understanding the user’s perspective

To narrow the topic down I did desk research and interviewed people about music, mental health and the role music plays in their lives. What struck me most was a theme appearing in every interview: the connection of certain music to certain stages of people’s lives, or to certain events.

Bands one listened to in their teens, birthday party music or a summer hit can not only bring nostalgia but also make you feel happy and warm, like a cosy sweater and hot chocolate. Music from the past was seen as a way to escape the worries of today into days that felt safer and happier.

The question

How might we combine music and memories for better mental health?

Brainstorming

I generated several potential ideas and sketched out user stories connected with my chosen direction.

Solution

Spotify Memories expands the idea of music as an escape from everyday life by bringing up people’s own memories. Here memories are treated as data we already generate - location, date and time, pictures, social activity, weather, fitness tracking and music - with playing music acting as the initiator of a memory event.

Say it’s your birthday. In the evening friends come over with presents, you turn on music, take pictures, dance. Data-wise, Spotify knows it’s your birthday, sees that you started a playlist and took photos, and your tracker logged some cardio. That rates as an event with high potential to become a memory - and can be given back to you later.

A wrapped year of memories

When the year is over, a user gets everything that happened to them in a familiar, shareable year-in-review format.
